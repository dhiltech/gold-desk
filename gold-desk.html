How the forecast works, and what it can’t do

Direction comes from four predictors with published research behind them: time-series momentum over 4 and 12 weeks (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, 2012), a 10-vs-40-week moving-average trend rule (the gold market-timing literature, e.g. Bartsch, Baur, Dichtl & Drobetz), an ARIMA(1,0,0) model of weekly returns (the classic statistical benchmark), and the 2-year base rate, since gold has risen in more weeks than it has fallen.

Ensemble weighting: each predictor’s vote is weighted by how far its own past hit rate sits above 50%. The weights are learned walk-forward, so the backtest never uses future data.

News comes from GDELT, a free global news index updated every 15 minutes. Each gold headline from the last 7 days is scored with a gold-specific keyword model: rate cuts, safe-haven demand, conflict, a weaker dollar and central-bank buying count as bullish; rate hikes, rising yields, a stronger dollar and price drops count as bearish. Research links macro and geopolitical news to both gold returns and volatility, so a spike in news volume also widens the forecast range. GDELT keeps only 3 months of articles, so the news signal’s track record covers about 12 weeks.

Combined call = price-model score × (1 − news weight) + news score × news weight. You set the news weight with the slider.

Range comes from a GARCH(1,1) volatility model fitted to daily returns, giving 68% and 90% bands for 7 days ahead. The centre is shifted by the average past move after similar calls.

Honest limits: one-week gold direction is close to a coin flip for everyone, including professional analysts, so always compare the hit rate against the “always up” baseline. Ranges are more reliable than direction. MCX here is derived from spot, not the exchange feed. Live price is PAX Gold (each token backed by one troy ounce of LBMA gold), which tracks spot closely; the feed falls back to spot sources automatically. This is an educational tool, not investment advice.
